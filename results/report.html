Adapter probes · sensitivity test

The Instance Term

The SelfIE-adapters paper reports that a learned bias vector accounts for ~85% of its improvement. That leaves open whether the model's self-interpretation reads the activation at all, or mostly recites a prior. Here we move the activation on purpose and watch what the description does.

Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct · layer 19 79,206 generations 1×A40 · 1h45m ↳ full protocol

The self-interpretation tracks the activation. Adding a steering direction v to an activation h moves the description toward that concept, monotonically and in both directions, while two controls that could have produced the same appearance stay flat. It responds at essentially the same intervention strength that changes the model's behaviour: λ₅₀ of 0.596 versus 0.576.

The effect is real and calibrated but small: λ explains 0.75% of description-embedding variance against 31.4% for which topic the activation came from. See effect size.

The sweep, and its controls

For each base activation h and unit direction v̂, we form f(h + λ‖h‖v̂), inject it as a soft token, and score the generated description against the concept using rank among a fixed 40-distractor pool — 0.5 is chance. Because the adapter L2-normalises its input, adding λv can only rotate the direction, never change its magnitude, so no norm confound can explain the result.

Why each control matters

Random direction — flat (0.498–0.509). This is the control that could have killed the result. If descriptions shifted as much for a norm-matched random vector, the effect would be a generic response to perturbation rather than to meaning. It doesn't.

Style direction — flat (0.500). The stronger control, and one we only knew to add after finding the confound below. A random direction is not something the model represents; the AxBench style direction is. Its flatness separates "responds to any real consistent direction" from "responds to this concept" — a question the random arm alone cannot answer.

Pure v — saturates instantly. Feeding λv with no h jumps to 0.796 at λ=0.3 and never moves. The concept arm instead climbs gradually (0.558 → 0.640 → 0.723), so h is still doing most of the work at low λ. By λ=2.0 the two converge and h has been washed out. The informative regime is λ ≲ 1; past that the model is merely describing v.

The prior, in its own words

Feeding the adapter a zero vector yields f(0) = b — the learned prior with no instance information whatsoever. It produces fluent, confident, specific descriptions:

b
the director of the 1967 film Blow-Up
b
the journalist who coined the term 'tropism'
b
the opera singer whose voice was used in the recording of the Hallelujah Chorus

They identify nothing: 0.0% recall@1. This is the sharpest qualification of the paper's own framing — "the bias accounts for ~85% of the improvement" is entirely compatible with the bias carrying zero instance information. It supplies format and register; the instance term does all the identifying work.

A confound that would have faked this result

AxBench's positive examples carry a concept-independent style signature. A classifier trained on some concepts and tested on held-out ones still separates positives from negatives at 0.969 AUC — "akin" appears in 17.2% of positives and 0.0% of negatives, and positives run 46% longer.

Left uncorrected, every steering vector would have been approximately the same vector, and the sweep would have shown a strong, direction-specific effect that passes the random-direction control — a false positive for the paper's faithfulness. Cross-concept mean-centring, the same trick the paper itself uses for topic vectors, removes it.

The λ effect is real, monotone, direction-specific and behaviourally calibrated — and it is small. Descriptions remain dominated by which topic h came from and by sampling noise at temperature 0.5. A reader who cares about magnitude rather than existence should weight this section over the curve.

What this does and doesn't establish

  • Not causal. Steering vectors are not clean semantic representations. This tests sensitivity to a controlled, independently validated change — nothing stronger.
  • Selection effect, declared. The ten concepts were chosen because they steer well and preserve fluency. These results describe the favourable case.
  • Narrow. Ten concepts, thirty topics, one model, one layer, one adapter architecture.
  • Topic displacement is partly generic. Similarity to the original topic also declines for the random and style arms, so not all of it is concept substitution.
  • Next step, out of scope here. Intervene on features or circuits whose causal role is independently established, and test whether the self-report tracks that.
